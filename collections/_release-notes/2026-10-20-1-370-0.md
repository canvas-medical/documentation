Today's release includes the following updates:
<span class="tag-bugfix">bugfix</span>

- Internal services that authenticate with Canvas JWTs now return 401 rather than a server error when a request carries an invalid, expired, or malformed token.
- Fixed an issue where the FHIR Claim API's `created` search parameter did not filter results by the claim's date of service. Integrations that pull claims incrementally now get back only claims in the requested date range instead of every claim on each pass.
- Fixed an issue where filtering the Data Integration fax list by inbound number could show faxes from other numbers, and the same number could appear more than once in the dropdown. Each number now appears once, and selecting it shows only that number's faxes.
- Fixed an issue where fields a plugin filled in on a command added by another plugin did not appear until the page was refreshed. Those fields now show right away.

<span class="tag-sdk">sdk</span>

- Internal: dependency upgrades for known vulnerabilities (aiohttp, cryptography, Pillow, pypdf, requests, urllib3 and others), plus django-constance 4, django-oauth-toolkit 3 and protobuf 7. No user-facing behavior change.
- Plugins can now fax a single referral, imaging order, lab order, or letter, not just a whole note. The fax sends the same PDF as the built-in Fax action and appears in the document's Fax Event History. If no recipient is given, it goes to the referral's service provider or the imaging order's imaging center.
- The patient portal now limits how frequently login links and sign-in attempts can be requested from a single source, protecting patients from login-link spam and blocking account-enumeration attempts.
- Prescribers can now verify their identity with ID.me from the provider menu, replacing the scheduled video appointment previously needed before Surescripts enrollment. Most people finish in about five minutes, and anyone who already has a verified ID.me account only needs to sign in and give permission. A verified NPI is filled in on the staff record automatically.
- Patients can now download and print the requisitions for their own lab and imaging orders from the patient portal. The lab requisition is the same form clinicians print (or the Health Gorilla form for electronically sent orders), and the imaging requisition is the "Prepare for your imaging test" patient letter. A new Patient App setting, `PATIENT_PORTAL_REQUISITION_ORDER_STATES`, controls whether patients see open orders only (the default) or open and completed orders. Each download is logged in the Audit Report and sends a `PATIENT_PORTAL__DOCUMENT_DOWNLOADED` plugin event.
- Internal: the 7 skipped patient demographics registration Cypress tests run in CI again. No user-facing change.
- Internal maintenance ahead of the Django 5.2 upgrade: updated the cross-origin request (CORS) library. No user-facing change.
- Installing or updating a plugin with a namespace access key that its namespace doesn't recognize now fails right away with a clear error. Before, the key was saved and the plugin quietly failed to load.
- Security hardening for plugins: plugins can no longer change the email or phone number on staff accounts, and patient filters in banner alerts and protocol cards are limited to supported patient fields. Existing plugins that use standard filters keep working.
- Administrators can now let users manage OAuth applications and upload, activate, or switch protocol versions by adding the new "Can register and manage OAuth applications" or "Can upload, activate, and switch versions of protocol code" permission to any group. Users no longer need a developer role for this. Users with the Administrative Developer or Clinical Developer role keep their current access.
- Several record-deletion and export endpoints check permissions more strictly, and sign-in tokens are no longer written to request logs.
- Patient portal booking, cancelling, and rescheduling now follow the same rules on the server that the portal shows.
- FHIR API: tokens with a patient context are refused on FHIR operations, can only author their own QuestionnaireResponses, can only link Observations to their own observations, cannot change staff-managed Patient fields or other patients' contact details through Patient update, and no longer receive staff login usernames on Practitioner.
- Patient portal and integration endpoints enforce more of their access rules on the server: patient documents open without putting the sign-in token in the link, and a patient's records and saved cards are reachable only by that patient.
- Staff limited to certain patient groups now see only those patients' records everywhere in the app, including allergies, conditions, care plans, care teams, documents, and order searches.
- Tightened staff security: creating, editing, or deleting staff accounts now requires staff management permissions, non-root admins can only view and change customer settings, and integration credentials and webhook secrets are no longer returned to the browser. Break-glass access no longer grants the ability to change roles, sign-in devices, or other access controls.
- Security improvements across the patient portal and staff admin: stricter consent handling in the portal, safer spreadsheet exports and document rendering, tighter permission checks on CCDA and admin actions, and outbound document and SIEM requests now limited to public destinations.
- Integrations using a read-only Practitioner token (such as Canvas Studio) can read the staff list again. That response leaves out staff tax IDs, and every other staff and API endpoint still requires full system access.
- Canvas Studio's service accounts are now set up with tighter, least-privilege permissions. A new protected "studio-api" account appears in the user list, and instance admins can't disable, rename, or delete it.
- Users with model or patient-group permissions enabled now see and change only the patients, notes, documents, coverages, messages, and other records they have access to. Appointment details for patients outside their groups are hidden.
- Billing actions now enforce role permissions. Changing claims, collecting payments, managing payers, and triggering ERA processing require revenue access and the matching permissions, and are limited to patients the user can access.
- Security hardening for login, MFA, the patient portal, and the OAuth/FHIR API: note and letter writes now require a write scope, post-login redirects only go to the same site, password-reset requests no longer show whether a contact exists, and MFA is enforced on real-time connections.
